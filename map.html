What the agreement work runs on

Part one showed the steps a contract goes through and who runs each one. Every one of those steps runs on knowledge, and knowledge lives in three places: outside the company, in its systems, or in its people. This page sorts the eight kinds by where they live and names what has to exist before a machine can carry a step.

01
Where it lives

Knowledge lives in three places

Everything a step runs on is something somebody knows: what the law requires, what the company has signed before, which positions it will and will not accept, who is allowed to bind it, and what happened the last time it dealt with this party. A person holds knowledge. When the organization holds the same material in a form the next lawyer can find without asking, it is intelligence.

Where the knowledge sits today decides how you get it. Each of the three places sets a different job: keep up with it, extract it, or capture it before it walks out the door.

Outside the company

Someone else maintains it

Laws, court decisions, industry standards, and market data on what terms are normal. The outside world keeps it current, and your counterparty rents the same copy.

Keep up with it
In the company's systems

Retrievable without asking a person

The agreements themselves, in the repository and the signing platform, with the terms pulled out of them. The playbook, once it is written down. Who is allowed to sign, in the systems that own that record.

Extract it, or read it live from the source
In its people

Retrievable only by asking, and it leaves when they do

Why the deal mattered. What got conceded in the last negotiation with this party, and what it bought. How the agreement turned out once it was live. Almost none of it gets written down, and it is most of what the department knew about the deal.

Capture it as it happens, under rules for who can see it
02
The kinds

The eight kinds, in the order a deal meets them

Every deal starts inside what the law requires and what the market treats as normal. Then a request comes in, and there is a reason behind it. The lawyers pull up what the company has signed before, apply the positions they have settled on, and negotiate the gaps with the other side. The right person signs, and how the deal plays out teaches the next one.

That walk touches all eight kinds, and each card below names one: what it is, which steps run on it, where it lives, and what shape its data is in today. Three of the eight exist only while the work is happening. Business context can be asked for at intake. Negotiation history needs what got said in the room, and almost nothing writes that down in a form the department keeps. Outcome data needs someone to go back after signing and record how the deal turned out, and few departments do. The other five are mostly a matter of extracting and maintaining what already exists. Each card lists the steps it feeds; the steps themselves are laid out in part one.

03
The data

The record each kind needs

A machine can run on a kind of knowledge only once that knowledge exists as a record. Part one made that the test for every step. The table applies it to the eight kinds, grouped by where each one lives, since the holder decides the job: the outside world's records are kept for you, the systems' records exist once someone extracts the terms or wires the source, and the records in people exist only once someone writes them down. Each row is the record that kind needs before a machine can carry the steps that run on it.

KindThe record it needs
The work runs on knowledge. A machine can carry a step only when what that step needs is written down somewhere it can read. The outside world's share is on record already, and so is most of what sits in the company's systems. The share held in people is not, and the steps that run on it stay with people until someone writes it down.
