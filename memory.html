What the work remembers

A legal department remembers in many places: the repository, the playbook, the inbox, and its people. A machine can only work from what it can read, so most of that memory has to be moved out of where it sits before the machine can use it, and once it is moved, someone owns it.

01
Where it lives

Memory lives in many places, and the machine can read from few of them

The repository holds what was signed and the playbook holds what the department will accept. What got conceded is in the inbox, the fact that an exception was granted is in the approval record, and the reason it was granted is in a senior lawyer's head. Each piece is memory, and each sits somewhere different.

For a machine to carry more of the agreement work, that memory has to come out of where it sits and into a form the machine can read. Plenty of companies are working on that from different directions. Platforms pull terms out of signed documents, drafting tools learn positions, assistants save preferences, and teams write their playbooks as rules.

Each of those brings in one kind of memory from one place. How far the machine can go depends on how much of the memory gets brought in, and whether a person can read it afterwards. The machine takes a step when the memory behind that step is in a form it can read and someone can show it got the step right. So what gets brought in is a step a lawyer stops touching, and what stays in someone's head keeps the step there with it.

In the repository

What was signed

Every executed agreement and amendment, with the terms pulled out of it. A machine can read this today, and most of the work so far has gone here.

In the playbook

What the department will accept

Positions, fallbacks, walk-aways, most of them in a document and some in a senior lawyer's head. The reasons behind them are mostly in the head.

The reasons and the results

Why this deal mattered, what got conceded and what it bought, why an exception got a yes, and whether the deal held up once it was live. Ask about any of it and you get a name.

02
How it gets in

Four ways memory gets into a machine

The four differ in what they cost the department, how much control it keeps, and who ends up owning what the machine learned.

ONE

Tell it

Write the positions as rules.

What ships

Every tool. This is the playbook, made machine-readable.

The catch

A rule with no reason attached decays into a rule nobody can maintain. Write each position with its author, its date, and its why.

TWO

Show it

Correct its output and let the correction stick.

What ships

Saying "remember this" and seeing it saved. No tool says how an edit becomes a memory.

The catch

A correction is cheap and carries no reason, so ask for the reason while the correction is being made, or it never gets written down.

THREE

Fill it in

A field filled in while the work happens, for why the deal matters, what got conceded, why the exception got a yes, and how it turned out.

What ships

One free-text box on the exception request. Engineering teams have kept decision records like this since 2011.

The catch

The field takes an afternoon. The habit takes a year, and needs an owner, a shared vocabulary, and rules for what gets recorded and who can see it.

FOUR

Let it watch

It infers what you prefer from what you do.

What ships

Most pitches that say "learns how you work" mean this.

The catch

It learns your ratified positions and your bad habits with the same appetite, and keeps no record of which one fired. What it learned carries no signature and no expiry date.

The memory you bring in yourself builds your team's judgment. What a tool picks up from watching you work goes into a store the department does not control, and no one on your side signed off on it, because there was never anything to sign. Ask any vendor where the learned patterns live, whether they leave with you, and who ratified them. You can buy the recorder. You can't buy the recordings.

Where to start. Three things, in this order, and none of them needs a new platform. Write each playbook position with an author, a date, and the reason behind it. Put a field for the reason on the exception request, and make it required. Keep the corrections lawyers make to the machine's drafts somewhere the machine can read them back. The first takes a quarter, the second an afternoon, and the third is a setting on most tools; the habit of filling them in is the year's work.

A legal department's memory sits in its systems and its people, and a machine can use the part it can read. Whatever is left in a person's head stays out of the machine. Every piece that gets written down is a step the machine can carry, and what the machine picks up by watching belongs to no one until someone signs it. The department that writes its memory down owns what the machine learns.
