The agreement work, rewired

This is not all of in-house legal. It is the lawyers who work on agreements, and today one of them touches every step of every contract the company signs. AI is about to change that, but not who answers for the result.

01
The work

What the agreement work is

The lawyers who work on agreements do two jobs at once.

The contract engine

It turns out the NDAs, sales agreements, and vendor contracts the business runs on.

The risk gate

It is the last read before the company signs its name to something it has to live with.

Nearly all of it is the same few moves, thousands of times a year: a request comes in, and an agreement gets drafted, negotiated, approved, signed, and one day renewed or ended. Today a lawyer touches every one of them, even the standard NDA that comes back signed with nothing changed. That is what AI changes. The question is which of those steps still needs a lawyer's hands on it.

02
The one idea

The agreement work comes in three kinds

Not all of it is the same, and that is the whole key. Some of it is routine, with one right answer. Some of it turns on judgment. And a little of it is a moment where a person has to answer for what happened.

Which kind it is decides where the lawyer stands. As AI takes the routine, the familiar human in the loop splits into three. Over the loop, the lawyer sets the rules once and sees only the exceptions. On the loop, they watch from the edge and step in on the calls that matter. In the loop, they keep their hands on the work and sign it. The riskier the work, the closer in they stand, the less rope the machine gets, and the more they are on the hook.

Routine

Watch from over the loop

The routine, high-volume work with one right answer. You set the rules and watch the system, not every file.
LawyerWrites the playbook, checks a sample, and watches the dashboard. You were not there for each one, and you can still answer for all of them, because you wrote the standard and you audit the results.
AgentDoes the work and kicks anything odd up to a person.
Logging and routing a new request, the conflicts check on a new counterparty, the routine markup on a standard order form, filing the signed copy, and watching the renewal dates.
Judgment

Sit on the loop

The work where the right answer depends on who is across the table and how much risk the company will take. You are in the decision before it goes out.
LawyerWeighs the options, makes the call, and writes down the reasoning.
AgentDrafts the options and flags where the other side is pushing.
How hard to push on the liability cap, whether to bend on indemnification, spotting the one buried clause that shifts real risk, and the give-and-take that lands the deal.
Accountable

Stay in the loop

The moments where a person has to answer for what happened. You own and sign each one, and you are on the hook.
LawyerMakes the call, signs, and answers for it. This cannot be handed to the agent.
AgentGets the package ready and checks it is complete. It never approves or signs.
Signing the contract that binds the company for years, approving the discount that sets a precedent, cutting off a vendor for breach, the board minutes, and the sign-off a regulator will read.

04
The catch

Why a lawyer never fully leaves

Agreement work is not generic operations work. A lawyer carries legal duties that a tool cannot take on. Those duties are the reason a lawyer stays close to the work wherever the stakes are highest, no matter how good the AI gets.

The duty to answer

A company lawyer owes the company real legal duties: loyalty, care, competence, confidentiality, honesty. An agent can do the work, but it cannot carry the duty. So the highest-stakes calls stay with a person who answers for them.

The bar rises with the tool

Once you have a tool that reliably catches a problem, ignoring what it finds is worse than never having looked. Being able to catch it creates a duty to act on it. So when the system raises a flag, you resolve it. You do not leave it sitting there as a record that you knew.

What this changes. For a lawyer, good and fast is only half the question. The rest is whether they can stand behind the work, whether it is protected, and who answers for it. That is what pushes the high-duty work closer in, and it shapes the records you keep.

AI takes the routine, a lawyer keeps the judgment and the sign-off, and the line between them moves as the record proves what can safely run on its own. That is what "AI-native" means: the lawyer is not replaced but moved to where they are worth the most, with the guardrails and records that let the machine handle the rest safely.
